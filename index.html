How to Hunt for Planet X with SPHEREx

1. The Auto-Blink Comparator: Alternates between Epoch 1 (Month 0) and Epoch 2 (Month 6). Distant background stars stay pinned, but nearby high-proper-motion brown dwarfs or outer solar system bodies visibly jump!
2. Mathematical Difference Subtraction (ΔI = E2 - E1): Subtracts stationary stars to neutral zero. Moving targets leave a dipole anomaly: a cyan deficit where it was in Epoch 1, and a magenta excess where it moved in Epoch 2.
3. 102-Band Spectrogram Fingerprinting: Cold planetary bodies and Y-dwarfs peak strongly in thermal IR (4.5–5.0 µm, Band 6) with deep methane absorption at 3.3 µm (Band 4). Normal stars peak at shorter NIR wavelengths (0.75–1.6 µm).
4. Optical Baseline Cross-Matching: Planet X and cold brown dwarfs are completely invisible in optical surveys (DSS2) and absent from Gaia DR3 optical astrometry. If an infrared dipole has no Gaia counterpart, it is a top discovery candidate!
